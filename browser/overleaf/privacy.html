shieldPrivacy & Controls

Last updated: November 2026 · Meld Research and its Overleaf connector, version 0.6.5

info_outline About Meld Research

Meld Research is designed to find meaningful collaborators for your research. The app builds a personal profile based on your private Overleaf and other data. This personal profile is stored on your own machine (and, with encrypted sync, as an end-to-end encrypted copy that only your passphrase or recovery key can unlock) and is then used to find collaborators with similar interests, share news updates and answer questions.

Private does not mean entirely on-device. Your indexed research and Updates are stored on your computer, but selected source text is analyzed by Azure AI Foundry through Meld's remote service. Nothing is processed until you sign in, give explicit consent, and select a project.

storage Where Your Data Goes

  • Activity log: the desktop companion keeps a log of its own activity (for example checks, processing progress and errors) in memory and in a small file on your computer, shown under More → Log. It names projects only by their Overleaf identifier and contains no research text or credentials; it is not uploaded.
  • Your computer: the desktop companion stores source text, your private profile (each research project's summary, details with their quoted sources, and changelog), and installation credentials in an encrypted research store, plus project snapshots you download. The research store is never uploaded in readable form.
  • Encrypted sync: when Meld's service offers it, you choose a passphrase in the desktop companion so your computers share one profile and a second computer continues from the first one's work instead of sending your projects to Azure AI Foundry again. Your included projects' research (processing state and the source text it was made from), your research projects' summaries, details and recent changelog, your include/exclude choices, skipped work, project summaries and OpenAlex match are encrypted on your computer and uploaded through short-lived links to Azure Blob Storage operated for Meld Research. Neither the Meld website nor Azure can read them: the passphrase never leaves your computer.
  • Overleaf: the connector reads projects through your normal signed-in browser session. Meld does not ask for your Overleaf password or export your login cookies.
  • Gmail (optional): only if you connect a Gmail account under Account → Gmail and agree. Google then gives the desktop companion read-only access (the gmail.readonly permission); Meld cannot send, change or delete mail. Meld uses only threads you wrote in (chats excluded), starting with the last 30 days, and looks for new mail in them about once an hour. Of each thread with new mail it sends only the new messages (signatures and quoted replies removed; mailing-list and automated mail only if you wrote it), with up to four earlier messages for context, through Meld's service to Azure AI Foundry, one thread at a time. A new thread is sent with short descriptions of your research projects (title, summary and first details) so it can be placed. A thread that belongs to a research project becomes one of its sources: its new messages are sent with that project's current details, which the model updates with quotes from the messages, and the change appears in your Updates feed. Threads that fit none of your projects wait in Private updates, with a short quoted preview, until you skip them, add them to a project or start a new one; Meld never starts a project on its own. Threads that are not work are skipped. Meld's server does not store the email text; the details, previews and quotes and the list of threads already analyzed are kept in the encrypted research store and your end-to-end encrypted sync. Google's access grant is kept only in the encrypted research store and in your end-to-end encrypted sync; it is never sent to Meld's server, put into a backup or logged. Meld's server provides only the Google sign-in client. While Meld Research is in Google's testing phase, Google asks you to reconnect every 7 days. Meld's use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy (developers.google.com/terms/api-services-user-data-policy), including the Limited Use requirements: it is used only for these features, not sold, not used for advertising or model training, and not read by people.
  • Azure AI Foundry: after you include a project, the companion reads it as document trees (a main document with the files it includes) and sends the trees edited in the last six months, oldest first, in parts of at most 60,000 characters, together with the research project's current details and its main paper's title and abstract, through the configured Meld service to the configured Azure AI Foundry chat deployment. Later checks send only documents that changed. The details are sent with the dates of their sources. The model returns edits to the details (at most 30 sentences), each citing lines that the service copies from the sent text; a two-sentence summary is then written from the details. At most once a month, a research project's details (with their sources' names and dates, but no source text) are also sent to merge overlapping details and retire plans that newer details overtook. To show the projects of one piece of research (for example a paper, its drafts and slides) as one research project, the summaries and first details of your research projects are also sent to decide which belong together, and combining two of them sends both detail lists. Processing runs in the background without a separate prompt for each model request.
  • The Meld website: keeps your account and approved installations in its own database. The remote Meld application does not persist a project archive or log research payloads. To monitor cost and capacity, it records for each model request only its time, deployment, purpose, outcome, token counts and duration, not its content or which account made it, and deletes these records after about 13 months.
  • Google: authenticates your Meld account. Google sign-in occurs in the normal browser; Meld does not ask for your Google password. If your Google account has a profile picture, the Meld website downloads it from Google when you sign in and stores it encrypted with your account, so the website and the desktop companion show it without contacting Google.
  • Project summaries: only when you choose Summarize for a project updated in the last six months, the connector reads that project and the companion sends only its main .tex file (the one with \documentclass and \begin{document}) through the Meld service to Azure AI Foundry for a one-paragraph description. This also applies to projects you have not included. The paragraph, the file's path and its hash are stored in the encrypted research store; the file text is not kept, and an unchanged main file is not sent again.
  • OpenAlex: only when you choose Search OpenAlex, the name you enter is sent through the Meld service to Azure AI Foundry (to suggest spellings and rank real candidates) and to the public OpenAlex catalog (openalex.org) to find author profiles. After you confirm an author, that author's public works are read from OpenAlex and stored in the encrypted research store on your computer, and refreshed weekly. The Meld service does not keep these searches or works.

description Data Meld Research Handles

  • Overleaf website content: project IDs, titles, last-updated values, archived/deleted flags, and ZIP contents of projects you select. Project contents may include personal or confidential information you or your collaborators put in them.
  • Overleaf tab information: the connector checks matching Overleaf tab URLs and loading state to reuse a tab or detect completion of sign-in. It does not record your general browsing history or inspect unrelated websites.
  • Overleaf project history: for an included project, the connector reads the project's Overleaf edit history inside the Overleaf tab and passes on only each file's path and the time it was last edited, so files can be processed in the order they were written. Collaborator names, emails and the edits themselves are discarded in the tab.
  • Overleaf sign-in status: while the connector popup is open, it requests your Overleaf project page to learn only whether Overleaf redirects to its sign-in page. The page content is discarded unread and nothing is sent to Meld.
  • A local preference: whether the connector is connected or disconnected is saved in this browser profile. Connection state is held in memory.
  • Research processing: UTF-8 .tex, .md and .txt source text of included projects, file paths, hashes and last-edit times, which files include which, and for each research project its summary, details with their quoted sources and changelog. A source you remove from a research project waits in Private updates until you add it to another project or skip it. Bibliographies, PDF and image contents are not analyzed by this version.
  • Profile choices and publications: whether you included or excluded each Overleaf project in your private profile, the name you searched on OpenAlex, the offered author candidates, the author you confirmed, and that author's public OpenAlex works (titles, authors, venues, years, DOIs and available abstracts).
  • Meld account and device information: a verified Google account identifier, name, email and profile picture, approved installation names, and credential/expiry records. The website uses cookies needed for account sign-in and request protection, and remembers in your browser's local storage only that this browser has been used with a Meld account (to offer "Sign in" instead of "Sign up"). The local app also uses request-protection cookies. The website's sign-up and sign-in pages load Google's sign-in script from accounts.google.com, so Google receives those page visits; Google then gives Meld a signed token with your account identifier, name and verified email address. Other Meld pages do not load Google scripts.

Project-list reads occur when the local Meld page connects or reconnects, after sign-in recovery, or when you choose Refresh projects. For included projects the desktop companion reads the project once a day, or at once when you choose Check now, and downloads a snapshot only when Overleaf reports a change. It does not automatically ingest the whole Overleaf account.

The browser sends its normal authentication cookies to Overleaf for these requests. The connector does not use a cookies permission, read or decrypt browser cookie databases, export session tokens, or send authentication cookies to the desktop companion. The complete project-list HTML page and unused account metadata are not exported from the tab.

check_circle_outline What You Approve

Signing in to the desktop companion opens the Meld website in your browser and returns a one-time sign-in to the companion on your computer that started it. If your Google account has no Meld account yet, the website asks before creating one. Including a project in your private profile starts monitoring it and requires explicit consent to send its selected text to Azure AI Foundry. Projects updated in the last six months each need your active choice to include or exclude them; older projects are only included if you choose them. Searching OpenAlex and confirming an author are separate choices of their own. If this notice changes materially, Meld asks for consent again before processing continues.

Allowlisted project metadata and selected ZIP bytes are transferred to the Meld Research companion on the same computer through Chrome native messaging. The companion uses them to display projects, validate ZIP contents, and read supported source text without extracting or executing archive files. It does not edit your Overleaf originals or automatically share snapshots with collaborators.

Research accuracy: the model selects evidence from supplied source lines and the service copies those lines into citations. Model interpretations can still be wrong. Check the cited evidence before relying on an update; a citation is not a guarantee of a correct scientific conclusion. Only process research you are authorized to send to these services.

visibility Who Can Access Your Research

Your Updates are not published to other Meld users, colleagues, or collaborators. Each linked account's local research is kept separate, and the website authorizes every request for the installation that sends it. Only the research Updates workflow is enabled with your Overleaf projects and, if you connect them, your Gmail accounts (read-only) and your public OpenAlex record: there is no WorkIQ integration, other news source, or alternative inference provider.

The Meld Research application does not sell research data or use it for advertising, unrelated analytics, model training, creditworthiness, or lending. Provider-side handling is governed by the configured Azure AI Foundry service and applicable Microsoft terms; this notice does not promise provider-side zero retention.

Provider roles: Google supplies account authentication and, if you connect it, read-only access to your Gmail; Overleaf supplies the projects you are authorized to access; OpenAlex supplies public author and publication metadata; Azure AI Foundry supplies model inference. Using these providers does not imply their endorsement. Their own terms and privacy practices apply to their services. Overleaf and any identity provider you choose during its normal sign-in handle requests under their own privacy policies. Chrome and the Chrome Web Store separately handle browser services, extension delivery, and updates.

lock Storage, Retention, and Protection

The extension does not persist project contents in browser storage. The desktop companion keeps its encrypted research store until you remove it. The remote service keeps account and device records in its own database. Names, email addresses, and device names in that database are encrypted with ASP.NET Data Protection, and installation credentials are stored only as one-way hashes. Account data remains until you choose Leave Meld Research (see below) or ask Markus Mobius to delete it. Installation credentials expire after 30 days unless revoked earlier.

Manual ZIP downloads and their metadata remain separate from the research index. Temporary transfer files are normally removed after completion or failure; a crash can leave temporary data on disk until it is removed. ZIP exports and existing manually downloaded archives are ordinary files and are not encrypted by Meld. The same applies to Download my data: it saves your research, including text from your Overleaf projects, as readable JSON in a ZIP file that Meld does not encrypt or keep track of.

Requests to hosted Overleaf use HTTPS. Native messaging uses operating-system pipes; the native host and local backend authenticate each other with per-run cryptographic proofs. Production requests to the remote service and requests to Foundry use HTTPS. Isolated local development can use a loopback HTTP account/inference service. The research store uses AES-GCM with a key protected by Windows DPAPI or the macOS Keychain. The macOS implementation still needs live distribution testing. The local UI and native-host loopback WebSocket use HTTP/WS, not TLS; temporary incoming archives are not encrypted by Meld. Device encryption and backups are controlled by your device configuration.

Encrypted sync encrypts each project, and separately your choices, with AES-256-GCM under a random key that is created on your computer. That key is stored in Azure only wrapped by a key derived from your passphrase (PBKDF2-SHA256, 600,000 iterations) and by a random recovery key; on your computers it is kept inside the encrypted research store. The recovery key is also stored there encrypted under that key, so Account → Remote Sync can show it again on any of your computers that is already unlocked; nobody without the key can read it. Each encrypted file is bound to your account and its name, and project file names are keyed hashes, so the storage does not reveal project names. Meld's service and Azure can see only your account identifier, the number, sizes and times of the encrypted files, and a storage quota (500 MB per account by default). Meld cannot reset your passphrase or read your synced research; if you lose both the passphrase and the recovery key, the synced copy cannot be decrypted and can only be deleted. Encryption keeps the content private but cannot hide that you use the service. The encrypted copy remains until you exclude projects, start over, or leave Meld Research.

delete_outline Your Controls and Deletion

Pause monitoring stops a selected project's research processing. Disconnect account stops local processing and requests remote credential revocation, retrying if the service is unavailable. Forget local research removes that project's processing state and its sources from your research projects for the linked account. Removing a source from a research project's details takes it off every detail of that project. Excluding a project also deletes its encrypted synced copy. Removing a Gmail account (Account → Gmail) withdraws Meld's access at Google and removes the account and its email threads on all your computers; you can also withdraw access in your Google account's security settings. Start over (offered when the passphrase and recovery key are lost) deletes every encrypted synced copy of the account; research on the computer where you choose it is uploaded again under the new passphrase. Disconnecting an account does not delete its encrypted copy.

Under Account → Backup, Download my data saves everything Meld keeps for your account on this computer (choices, research projects with their details and changelog, publications and the Overleaf text they were made from) as readable JSON, and Upload data restores such a file into the signed-in account (also a new one); encrypted sync then uploads it. Under Account → Meld account, Leave Meld Research, after you type DELETE, deletes your Meld account on Meld's server with its devices, profile picture and encrypted synced research, erases the research on this computer, and uninstalls the installed app: its start at sign-in, its browser bridge registration, its Start menu entry, and its application and data folders. A short log of the removal is left in your temporary folder. Remove the browser connector yourself in Chrome or Edge. Other computers keep their local copy until you leave there too.

Disconnect in the connector popup, or Pause auto-connect in Meld, stops the browser connection and remains in effect after browser restarts. You can also disable or remove the extension in Chrome. These actions do not delete ZIPs already stored by the desktop companion.

To delete local research data, stop Meld and remove the relevant files in the downloads and incoming subdirectories of its data folder. To remove all accounts' local research indexes and credentials, stop Meld and remove the research subdirectory as well. Request remote credential revocation with Disconnect account before doing this when possible. A macOS Keychain key entry may remain; it contains a key, not project contents. Also delete ZIP copies or backups you saved elsewhere if you no longer need them.

  • Windows default: %LOCALAPPDATA%\MeldResearch\OverleafPrototype.
  • macOS default: ~/Library/Application Support/MeldResearch/OverleafPrototype. macOS distribution has not yet been live-verified.
  • An explicit test data-directory override changes these locations.

Removing the extension removes its local settings according to Chrome's normal extension storage behavior; it does not remove the companion's research store or revoke a Meld account. Your original Overleaf projects are unaffected by local deletion. A request already dispatched to Foundry cannot be recalled, although pausing cancels local in-progress work where possible.

receipt_long Local Logs and Development Mode

Application diagnostic logs omit project titles, contents, cookies, and credentials. They may record connection events and safe error codes.

Dummy development mode uses a clearly labelled synthetic account and synthetic Overleaf evidence, but real configured Foundry inference. It does not use a real Overleaf browser profile or perform real Google sign-in. Both app services may run on the developer's computer; real model calls still leave that computer and consume quota. A Dummy demonstration is not evidence that the public site, real Google login, or a browser-store release has been deployed or approved.
