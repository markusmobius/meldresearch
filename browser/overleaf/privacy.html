shieldPrivacy & Controls

Last updated: October 2026 · Meld Research and its Overleaf connector, version 0.5.0

info_outline About Meld Research

Meld Research is designed to find meaningful collaborators for your research. The app builds a personal profile based on your private Overleaf and other data. This personal profile is only stored on your own machine and is then used to find collaborators with similar interests, share news updates and answer questions.

Private does not mean entirely on-device. Your indexed research and Updates are stored on your computer, but selected source text is analyzed by Azure AI Foundry through Meld's remote service. Nothing is processed until you sign in, give explicit consent, and select a project.

storage Where Your Data Goes

  • Your computer: the desktop companion stores source text, project summaries (your private profile), Update history, and installation credentials in an encrypted research store, plus project snapshots you download. The research store is not uploaded to the Meld website.
  • Overleaf: the connector reads projects through your normal signed-in browser session. Meld does not ask for your Overleaf password or export your login cookies.
  • Azure AI Foundry: after you include a project, the companion sends it one file at a time (and, for a changed file, its previous version) together with the project's current summary through the configured Meld service to the configured Azure AI Foundry chat deployment. The updated summary and grounded Updates are returned to your computer. Processing runs in the background without a separate prompt for each model request.
  • The Meld website: keeps your account and approved installations in its own database. The remote Meld application does not persist a project archive or log research payloads.
  • Google: authenticates your Meld account. Google sign-in occurs in the normal browser; Meld does not ask for your Google password.
  • OpenAlex: only when you choose Search OpenAlex, the name you enter is sent through the Meld service to Azure AI Foundry (to suggest spellings and rank real candidates) and to the public OpenAlex catalog (openalex.org) to find author profiles. After you confirm an author, that author's public works are read from OpenAlex and stored in the encrypted research store on your computer, and refreshed weekly. The Meld service does not keep these searches or works.

description Data Meld Research Handles

  • Overleaf website content: project IDs, titles, last-updated values, archived/deleted flags, and ZIP contents of projects you select. Project contents may include personal or confidential information you or your collaborators put in them.
  • Overleaf tab information: the connector checks matching Overleaf tab URLs and loading state to reuse a tab or detect completion of sign-in. It does not record your general browsing history or inspect unrelated websites.
  • Overleaf project history: for an included project, the connector reads the project's Overleaf edit history inside the Overleaf tab and passes on only each file's path and the time it was last edited, so files can be processed in the order they were written. Collaborator names, emails and the edits themselves are discarded in the tab.
  • Overleaf sign-in status: while the connector popup is open, it requests your Overleaf project page to learn only whether Overleaf redirects to its sign-in page. The page content is discarded unread and nothing is sent to Meld.
  • A local preference: whether the connector is connected or disconnected is saved in this browser profile. Connection state is held in memory.
  • Research processing: UTF-8 .tex, .md and .txt source text of included projects, file paths, hashes and last-edit times, each project's summary (at most 5,000 characters), Updates and their citations. Bibliographies, PDF and image contents are not analyzed by this version.
  • Profile choices and publications: whether you included or excluded each Overleaf project in your private profile, the name you searched on OpenAlex, the offered author candidates, the author you confirmed, and that author's public OpenAlex works (titles, authors, venues, years, DOIs and available abstracts).
  • Meld account and device information: a verified Google account identifier, name and email, approved installation names, and credential/expiry records. The website uses cookies needed for account sign-in and request protection, and remembers in your browser's local storage only that this browser has been used with a Meld account (to offer "Sign in" instead of "Sign up"). The local app also uses request-protection cookies. The website's sign-up and sign-in pages load Google's sign-in script from accounts.google.com, so Google receives those page visits; Google then gives Meld a signed token with your account identifier, name and verified email address. Other Meld pages do not load Google scripts.

Project-list reads occur when the local Meld page connects or reconnects, after sign-in recovery, or when you choose Refresh projects. For included projects the desktop companion reads the project once a day, or at once when you choose Check now, and downloads a snapshot only when Overleaf reports a change. It does not automatically ingest the whole Overleaf account.

The browser sends its normal authentication cookies to Overleaf for these requests. The connector does not use a cookies permission, read or decrypt browser cookie databases, export session tokens, or send authentication cookies to the desktop companion. The complete project-list HTML page and unused account metadata are not exported from the tab.

check_circle_outline What You Approve

Signing in to the desktop companion opens the Meld website in your browser and returns a one-time sign-in to the companion on your computer that started it. If your Google account has no Meld account yet, the website asks before creating one. Including a project in your private profile starts monitoring it and requires explicit consent to send its selected text to Azure AI Foundry. Projects updated in the last six months each need your active choice to include or exclude them; older projects are only included if you choose them. Searching OpenAlex and confirming an author are separate choices of their own. If this notice changes materially, Meld asks for consent again before processing continues.

Allowlisted project metadata and selected ZIP bytes are transferred to the Meld Research companion on the same computer through Chrome native messaging. The companion uses them to display projects, validate ZIP contents, and read supported source text without extracting or executing archive files. It does not edit your Overleaf originals or automatically share snapshots with collaborators.

Research accuracy: the model selects evidence from supplied source lines and the service copies those lines into citations. Model interpretations can still be wrong. Check the cited evidence before relying on an update; a citation is not a guarantee of a correct scientific conclusion. Only process research you are authorized to send to these services.

visibility Who Can Access Your Research

Your Updates are not published to other Meld users, colleagues, or collaborators. Each linked account's local research is kept separate, and the website authorizes every request for the installation that sends it. Only the research Updates workflow is enabled with your Overleaf projects and, if you link it, your public OpenAlex record: there is no WorkIQ integration, other news source, or alternative inference provider.

The Meld Research application does not sell research data or use it for advertising, unrelated analytics, model training, creditworthiness, or lending. Provider-side handling is governed by the configured Azure AI Foundry service and applicable Microsoft terms; this notice does not promise provider-side zero retention.

Provider roles: Google supplies account authentication; Overleaf supplies the projects you are authorized to access; OpenAlex supplies public author and publication metadata; Azure AI Foundry supplies model inference. Using these providers does not imply their endorsement. Their own terms and privacy practices apply to their services. Overleaf and any identity provider you choose during its normal sign-in handle requests under their own privacy policies. Chrome and the Chrome Web Store separately handle browser services, extension delivery, and updates.

lock Storage, Retention, and Protection

The extension does not persist project contents in browser storage. The desktop companion keeps its encrypted research store until you remove it. The remote service keeps account and device records in its own database. Names, email addresses, and device names in that database are encrypted with ASP.NET Data Protection, and installation credentials are stored only as one-way hashes. Account data remains until removed by the service operator; contact Markus Mobius to request account deletion. There is not yet a self-service remote account-deletion screen. Installation credentials expire after 30 days unless revoked earlier.

Manual ZIP downloads and their metadata remain separate from the research index. Temporary transfer files are normally removed after completion or failure; a crash can leave temporary data on disk until it is removed. ZIP exports and existing manually downloaded archives are ordinary files and are not encrypted by Meld.

Requests to hosted Overleaf use HTTPS. Native messaging uses operating-system pipes; the native host and local backend authenticate each other with per-run cryptographic proofs. Production requests to the remote service and requests to Foundry use HTTPS. Isolated local development can use a loopback HTTP account/inference service. The research store uses AES-GCM with a key protected by Windows DPAPI or the macOS Keychain. The macOS implementation still needs live distribution testing. The local UI and native-host loopback WebSocket use HTTP/WS, not TLS; temporary incoming archives are not encrypted by Meld. Device encryption and backups are controlled by your device configuration.

delete_outline Your Controls and Deletion

Pause monitoring stops a selected project's research processing. Disconnect account stops local processing and requests remote credential revocation, retrying if the service is unavailable. Forget local research removes that project's summary, processing state, and update history for the linked account.

Disconnect in the connector popup, or Pause auto-connect in Meld, stops the browser connection and remains in effect after browser restarts. You can also disable or remove the extension in Chrome. These actions do not delete ZIPs already stored by the desktop companion.

To delete local research data, stop Meld and remove the relevant files in the downloads and incoming subdirectories of its data folder. To remove all accounts' local research indexes and credentials, stop Meld and remove the research subdirectory as well. Request remote credential revocation with Disconnect account before doing this when possible. A macOS Keychain key entry may remain; it contains a key, not project contents. Also delete ZIP copies or backups you saved elsewhere if you no longer need them.

  • Windows default: %LOCALAPPDATA%\MeldResearch\OverleafPrototype.
  • macOS default: ~/Library/Application Support/MeldResearch/OverleafPrototype. macOS distribution has not yet been live-verified.
  • An explicit test data-directory override changes these locations.

Removing the extension removes its local settings according to Chrome's normal extension storage behavior; it does not remove the companion's research store or revoke a Meld account. Your original Overleaf projects are unaffected by local deletion. A request already dispatched to Foundry cannot be recalled, although pausing cancels local in-progress work where possible.

receipt_long Local Logs and Development Mode

Application diagnostic logs omit project titles, contents, cookies, and credentials. They may record connection events and safe error codes.

Dummy development mode uses a clearly labelled synthetic account and synthetic Overleaf evidence, but real configured Foundry inference. It does not use a real Overleaf browser profile or perform real Google sign-in. Both app services may run on the developer's computer; real model calls still leave that computer and consume quota. A Dummy demonstration is not evidence that the public site, real Google login, or a browser-store release has been deployed or approved.
