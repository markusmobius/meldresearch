Privacy & controls

Privacy notice for Meld Research and its Overleaf connector, version 0.3.0. Publisher and responsible contact: Markus Mobius, Meld Research.

Meld Research is an independent project, not an internal Microsoft service or a Microsoft Research study. This notice covers the website and account service, the local desktop companion, the Overleaf connector, and the research processing described below. Contact markusmobius@gmail.com for privacy requests.

Use your existing sign-in

Read Overleaf through your normal browser. Meld does not ask for your Overleaf password or export your login cookies.

Choose what to monitor

Meld lists accessible projects locally. Account linking and explicit consent are required before monitoring selected projects.

Local storage, Foundry processing

Selected research text is sent through Meld's remote service to Azure AI Foundry. Indexed research and updates are stored on your computer.

Data Meld Research handles

  • Overleaf website content: project IDs, titles, last-updated values, archived/deleted flags, and ZIP contents of projects you select. Project contents may include personal or confidential information you or your collaborators put in them.
  • Overleaf tab information: the connector checks matching Overleaf tab URLs and loading state to reuse a tab or detect completion of sign-in. It does not record your general browsing history or inspect unrelated websites.
  • A local preference: whether auto-connect is enabled or paused is saved in this browser profile. Connection state is held in memory.
  • Research processing: selected UTF-8 source text, file paths and hashes, before/after snapshots, extracted facts, citations, and embeddings. PDF and image contents are not analyzed by this version.
  • Meld account and device information: a verified Google account identifier, name and email, approved installation names, and credential/expiry records. The website uses cookies needed for account sign-in and request protection, and remembers in your browser's local storage only that this browser has been used with a Meld account (to offer "Sign in" instead of "Sign up"). The local app also uses request-protection cookies. Google sign-in occurs in the normal browser; Meld does not ask for your Google password. The website's sign-up and sign-in pages load Google's sign-in script from accounts.google.com, so Google receives those page visits; Google then gives Meld a signed token with your account identifier, name and verified email address. Other Meld pages do not load Google scripts.

Project-list reads occur when the local Meld page connects or reconnects, after sign-in recovery, or when you choose Refresh projects. If you enable monitoring, the desktop companion periodically checks selected projects, normally every five minutes, and requests snapshots when needed. It also verifies unchanged timestamps periodically and supports an explicit content check. It does not automatically ingest the whole Overleaf account.

The browser sends its normal authentication cookies to Overleaf for these requests. The connector does not use a cookies permission, read or decrypt browser cookie databases, export session tokens, or send authentication cookies to the desktop companion. The complete project-list HTML page and unused account metadata are not exported from the tab.

How data is used and shared

Allowlisted project metadata and selected ZIP bytes are transferred to the Meld Research companion on the same computer through Chrome native messaging. The companion uses them to display projects, validate ZIP contents, and read supported source text without extracting or executing archive files. It does not edit your Overleaf originals or automatically share snapshots with collaborators.

After you link an existing Meld account and explicitly enable a project, the companion sends bounded research text, prior knowledge, and relevant changes through the configured Meld service to the configured Azure AI Foundry chat and embedding deployments. This is not on-device-only processing. The resulting grounded updates and vectors are returned to your computer. The remote Meld application does not persist a project archive or log research payloads.

The Meld Research application does not sell research data or use it for advertising, unrelated analytics, model training, creditworthiness, or lending. Provider-side handling is governed by the configured Azure AI Foundry service and applicable Microsoft terms; this notice does not promise provider-side zero retention. Only the research Updates workflow is enabled: there is no WorkIQ integration, other news source, or alternative inference provider.

Provider roles: Google supplies account authentication; Overleaf supplies the projects you are authorized to access; Azure AI Foundry supplies model inference. Using these providers does not make Meld Research an internal Microsoft project or imply their endorsement. Their own terms and privacy practices apply to their services.

Research accuracy: the model selects evidence from supplied source lines and the service copies those lines into citations. Model interpretations can still be wrong. Check the cited evidence before relying on an update; a citation is not a guarantee of a correct scientific conclusion.

Overleaf and any identity provider you choose during its normal sign-in handle requests under their own privacy policies. Chrome and the Chrome Web Store separately handle browser services, extension delivery, and updates. Contacting support voluntarily sends your message to the email provider used for the support address; do not include passwords, cookies, or confidential project files.

Storage, retention, and protection

The extension does not persist project contents in browser storage. The desktop companion stores indexed source text, project knowledge, embeddings, update history, and installation credentials in an encrypted research store until you remove them. The remote service keeps account and device records in its own database. Names, email addresses, and device names in that database are encrypted with ASP.NET Data Protection, and installation credentials are stored only as one-way hashes. Account data remains until removed by the service operator; contact Markus Mobius to request account deletion. There is not yet a self-service remote account-deletion screen. Installation credentials expire after 30 days unless revoked earlier.

Manual ZIP downloads and their metadata remain separate from the research index. Temporary transfer files are normally removed after completion or failure; a crash can leave temporary data on disk until it is removed. ZIP exports and existing manually downloaded archives are ordinary files and are not encrypted by Meld.

Requests to hosted Overleaf use HTTPS. Native messaging uses operating-system pipes; the native host and local backend authenticate each other with per-run cryptographic proofs. Production requests to the remote service and requests to Foundry use HTTPS. Isolated local development can use a loopback HTTP account/inference service. The research store uses AES-GCM with a key protected by Windows DPAPI or the macOS Keychain. The macOS implementation still needs live distribution testing. The local UI and native-host loopback WebSocket use HTTP/WS, not TLS; temporary incoming archives are not encrypted by Meld. Device encryption and backups are controlled by your device configuration.

Application diagnostic logs omit project titles, contents, cookies, and credentials. They may record connection events and safe error codes.

Dummy development mode uses a clearly labelled synthetic account and synthetic Overleaf evidence, but real configured Foundry inference. It does not use a real Overleaf browser profile or perform real Google sign-in. Both app services may run on the developer's computer; real model calls still leave that computer and consume quota. A Dummy demonstration is not evidence that the public site, real Google login, or a browser-store release has been deployed or approved.

Your controls and deletion

Pause monitoring stops a selected project's research processing. Disconnect account stops local processing and requests remote credential revocation, retrying if the service is unavailable. Forget local research removes that project's indexed sources, knowledge, and update history for the linked account.

Pause auto-connect in Meld or the connector popup stops the browser connection and remains paused after browser restarts. You can also disable or remove the extension in Chrome. These actions do not delete ZIPs already stored by the desktop companion.

To delete local research data, stop Meld and remove the relevant files in the downloads and incoming subdirectories of its data folder. To remove all accounts' local research indexes and credentials, stop Meld and remove the research subdirectory as well. Request remote credential revocation with Disconnect account before doing this when possible. A macOS Keychain key entry may remain; it contains a key, not project contents. Also delete ZIP copies or backups you saved elsewhere if you no longer need them.

  • Windows default: %LOCALAPPDATA%\MeldResearch\OverleafPrototype.
  • macOS development path: ~/Library/Application Support/MeldResearch/OverleafPrototype. macOS distribution has not yet been live-verified for this prototype.
  • An explicit test data-directory override changes these locations.

Removing the extension removes its local settings according to Chrome's normal extension storage behavior; it does not remove the companion's research store or revoke a Meld account. Your original Overleaf projects are unaffected by local deletion. A request already dispatched to Foundry cannot be recalled, although pausing cancels local in-progress work where possible.
