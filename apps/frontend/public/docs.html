System Overview

Meld Research follows the Overleaf projects you choose and turns meaningful changes into a research Updates feed. Each Update is grounded in cited lines from your own sources. Your private research comes only from Overleaf, read through your normal signed-in browser. Meld Research is developed by Markus Mobius.

A local assistant and a remote service work together:

computer

Local Backend (Meld Research Assistant)

Runs on your computer and serves the green local app. It reads the projects you select through the Overleaf connector in Chrome or Edge, keeps an encrypted research index, and sends bounded source text through the Meld service to Azure AI Foundry for analysis.

Your project files, extracted knowledge, embeddings, and Updates stay on your computer.

cloud

Remote Backend (www.meldresearch.org)

The blue website handles Google sign-up and sign-in, signed desktop releases, and authenticated requests to Azure AI Foundry. It keeps account and device records, not project archives or research payloads.

Your Daily Workflow

  • Feed: read new Updates from your monitored projects and review their cited evidence.
  • About me: your public publications from OpenAlex.
  • Account: sign in with Google, connect Overleaf, include or exclude projects, and link OpenAlex.

Azure AI Foundry Processing

Analysis is not on-device-only. After you sign in and consent for a project, selected text, prior knowledge, and relevant changes are sent through the Meld service to the configured Azure AI Foundry chat and embedding deployments. The service does not persist project archives or log research payloads. Provider-side handling is governed by Azure AI Foundry and applicable Microsoft terms.

Green and blue identify roles, not locations. The local app is green and the remote website is blue. In local development, both backends run on your computer; Foundry inference still uses the configured model service.

Installation Guide

Meld Research is split into a local assistant that runs on your computer and a remote website. Sign up with Google on https://www.meldresearch.org; the home page then shows Install Meld Research, which opens the installation card. The account menu at the top right also links to it.

Windows Installation

  • Option 1: Quick Install (Recommended): open PowerShell (no administrator rights needed) and run the one-liner shown on the page: 
    irm -useb https://www.meldresearch.org/api/install/windows | iex
    It downloads the signed release, checks its length and SHA-256, installs it under %LOCALAPPDATA%\MeldResearch\App, registers a scheduled task that starts Meld Research in the background when you sign in, and opens the local app.
  • Option 2: Manual Script Execution: download meldresearch-install.ps1 from the Manual tab, open PowerShell in the download folder, and run: 
    powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File .\meldresearch-install.ps1

macOS Installation

  • Option 1: Quick Install (Recommended): open Terminal and paste: 
    curl -fsSL https://www.meldresearch.org/api/install/macos | bash
    It installs under ~/Library/Application Support/MeldResearch/App and adds a LaunchAgent that starts Meld Research at login.
  • Option 2: Manual Script Execution: download meldresearch-install.sh, open Terminal in the download folder, and run: 
    bash meldresearch-install.sh

The packages are self-contained, so no separate .NET installation is needed. Running the command again repairs or updates an installation and keeps your research data. Installed copies update themselves from signed releases. On Windows, the Start menu entry Meld Research opens the app. These are test distributions, not yet signed or notarized installers.

After Installing

1

Add the Overleaf connector

The assistant registers its browser bridge; you still approve the extension in Chrome or Edge. While the store release is pending, use the documented unpacked-extension setup.

2

Sign in

Open Account in the local app and choose Sign in with Google. Your browser opens the Meld website; if you are already signed in there, it returns to the app immediately.

3

Sign in to Overleaf in your normal browser

Meld never asks for your Overleaf password or reads your browser's cookie database.

4

Choose projects and consent

In Account → Overleaf → Projects, review Privacy & Controls, and choose Include (your consent to process that project) or Exclude for each project updated in the last six months. Only process research you are authorized to send to these services.

5

Link OpenAlex (optional)

In Account → OpenAlex, search by your name; the candidates appear right away. After you confirm which author profile is you, Meld imports your public publications in the background (continuing after a restart and refreshing weekly), and they appear in About me.

Reading Your Updates

The Feed shows research Updates from the projects you monitor, newest first. There are no other feed types: no workplace news, colleague sharing, or other sources.

What an Update Contains

  • Update: a meaningful addition or revision found in a changed file.
  • Knowledge removed: a deleted or rewritten file that withdraws an earlier claim.
  • The first import of a project builds your private profile and produces no Updates: it describes what the project already says, not work that happened today.

Choose Review to inspect the complete cited evidence: the exact source lines behind each claim. Dates show when Meld observed the evidence. Check the cited lines before relying on an Update; a citation is not a guarantee that a model's interpretation is correct.

Checking for Changes

Included projects are checked once a day while Meld and your browser are running. The refresh button in the Feed, or Check now in Account → Overleaf → Projects, checks them at once. Unchanged content makes no model calls, and formatting-only edits can be analyzed without producing a new Update.

Projects & Consent

Account → Overleaf → Projects is where you control which research Meld processes. Nothing is monitored until you link your account, give explicit consent, and include a project. Every project updated in the last six months needs an active choice to include or exclude it; Meld opens this tab each time you load the app, and whenever a new project appears, until you have chosen. Older projects are optional. Your feed opens once one project is included.

  • Select Overleaf projects: the list comes from your signed-in Overleaf account through the connector. Archived and deleted projects are not included.
  • Consent: choosing Include allows that project's text to be sent through Meld's remote service to Azure AI Foundry for building your private profile and Updates. If the notice changes, Meld asks you to resume each project before continuing.
  • Pause: stop processing a project without deleting the knowledge already collected.
  • Forget local research: delete that project's local index and Updates for your account and exclude it from your profile. Overleaf originals and manual ZIP downloads are unchanged.
  • Download ZIP: save a local snapshot of a project. ZIP files are ordinary, unencrypted files.

Only UTF-8 .tex, .md, and .txt sources of up to 128 KB are analyzed. Bibliographies (.bib), PDFs, images and larger files are tracked by hash but not sent to the model. The Projects tab shows which project is importing or updating, how many files are done, and what is queued.

Account & Connector

Your Meld Account

Your Google account identifies you; Meld stores its identifier, name, and verified email address. On the website, the account menu at the top right shows who is signed in and offers Install and Sign out.

To sign in to the local app, open Account and choose Sign in with Google. The app opens the Meld website in your browser. If you are signed in there with a Meld account, it returns to the app at once; otherwise you sign in with Google, or create an account with one click, first. The website hands the sign-in back only to the app on your own computer that started it, so there is no code to check or request to approve. Disconnect account stops local processing and asks the website to revoke the app's credential, retrying if it is unreachable. App credentials expire after 30 days.

The Overleaf Connector

The connector is a browser extension that reads Overleaf through your normal signed-in session and passes the project list and selected project ZIPs to the local assistant through the browser's native messaging. You do not need to open its popup every day. Pause auto-connect stops the connection and remains paused after browser restarts. Google authentication for Meld and your Overleaf session are separate.

Local Backend (Meld Research Assistant)

The local assistant runs on your computer and serves the Meld Research app at http://127.0.0.1:6001. It coordinates AI processing; it does not run the models on your computer.

Core Responsibilities

  • Read Overleaf: request project lists and snapshots of selected projects from the connector, validate ZIP contents, and read supported source text without extracting or executing archive files.
  • Detect change: compare supported source content, not ZIP order or timestamps.
  • Build your profile: send one file at a time, with the current project summary, to Azure AI Foundry and keep the updated summary and any Updates.
  • Keep itself current: install signed releases side by side and restart into them, returning to the previous version if a new one does not start.

Three Different Data Boundaries

Your computer holds project snapshots, the research index, and Updates. Azure AI Foundry receives bounded source text through the Meld service for analysis. The Meld website stores your account and approved installations, not your research.

State Storage

The research store is encrypted with AES-GCM using a key protected by Windows DPAPI or the macOS Keychain. Default data folders are %LOCALAPPDATA%\MeldResearch\OverleafPrototype on Windows and ~/Library/Application Support/MeldResearch/OverleafPrototype on macOS. Manual ZIP downloads and temporary transfers in that folder are not encrypted by Meld; treat them as sensitive.

Remote Backend (www.meldresearch.org)

The remote website at https://www.meldresearch.org handles accounts and authenticated model requests. It does not keep a repository of project archives.

What the Website Stores

  • Accounts: your Google account identifier, name, and verified email. Names, emails, and device names are encrypted in its database.
  • Installations: approved device names and credential records; credentials are stored only as one-way hashes and expire after 30 days unless revoked earlier.
  • Desktop releases: signed packages and manifests for the one-line installers and automatic updates.

Processing

The local assistant sends research requests with its installation credential. The website checks the credential and forwards bounded requests to the configured Azure AI Foundry deployments using the service's own Azure credentials, then returns the results. Foundry credentials never reach your computer or browser, and research payloads are not logged.

Remote Website

Home shows a single action: sign up or sign in with Google, then Install Meld Research. The website is not a copy of your private local Feed or About me.

Background Processing

The local assistant works through a queue for the projects you included. Processing waits for sign-in and consent; pausing or disconnecting stops it.

1. Read Included Projects

Each included project is read once a day, or at once when you choose Check now. Unchanged Overleaf metadata needs no download. The connector also reads, from the project's Overleaf history, when each file was last edited (paths and times only).

2. Compare Source Content

Supported files are hashed. Files whose content the summary already reflects, and identical copies in other folders, cause zero model calls, including after a restart.

3. Summarize File by File

Changed files are queued, earliest edited first. Each step sends one file and the project's current summary (at most 5,000 characters) to Azure AI Foundry, which returns the updated summary; after the first import it also reports each meaningful change as an Update.

4. Commit Each Step

Every file step saves the summary, the file's hash and its Updates together, so a restart resumes at the next file. A failed step changes nothing and is retried later without blocking other projects. Pausing, forgetting, or disconnecting during processing prevents an in-flight result from restoring removed data.

Failures, Offline Use, and Visibility

Failures are shown on the project in Account → Overleaf → Projects rather than replaced by invented results. Previously collected Updates remain readable when Overleaf or the network is unavailable.

Novelty & Citations

Foundry decides what is new by comparing each changed file with the current project summary. No local heuristic replaces the model's decision.

auto_awesome

Novelty

The first import builds the summary. Later steps compare a file's before and after text with the summary and report additions, revisions, or removals, or nothing at all.

format_quote

Grounded citations

The model selects source line ranges; the service copies their exact text into citations and validates them. Invalid references are rejected visibly.

Evidence limits keep requests bounded: currently 128 KiB per file and 256 KiB per project. Citations make each claim inspectable; they do not guarantee that an interpretation is correct.

Development & Dummy Mode

From the repository root, use the full-stack launcher with .NET 10, Node 24/npm, and PowerShell 7:

.\run-dev.ps1 -Mode Dummy
.\run-dev.ps1 -Mode Dummy -SkipBuild
.\run-dev.ps1 -Mode Live
.\run-dev.ps1 -Check

Dummy: a clearly synthetic account and Overleaf source, but real configured Foundry chat and embeddings. It consumes model quota and does not use a real Overleaf profile.

Live: real Overleaf through your normal browser and real Google sign-in. Missing credentials are errors, not simulated success.

Keep the launcher terminal open; Ctrl+C stops its own processes. An occupied port is a conflict, not permission to stop another application. Dummy data and logs persist under .local-test\Dummy.

The repository's README.md documents Foundry and Google configuration, deployment, and releases; AGENTS.md records the project direction for future development. Never put tokens, Google secrets, or private Overleaf evidence into source control.

Limitations & Status

  • Supported research text: UTF-8 .tex, .md, and .txt. .bib files are tracked by hash only. PDFs and images are not analyzed.
  • Hosted www.overleaf.com, normal Chrome or Edge profiles, and one connected profile at a time. Safari, Firefox, self-hosted Overleaf, editing, and live collaboration are not supported.
  • The Windows installation has been execution-tested. macOS installation still needs live Mac validation; signing, notarization, and store approval are separate release steps.
  • Indexed research is encrypted, but manual exports and temporary transfers are not all encrypted by Meld. Loopback transport is HTTP/WS. See Privacy & Controls for the complete disclosure.
